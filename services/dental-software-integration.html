The Problem

Your Software Works. It Just Does Not Work Together

Walk the front desk through one new patient. The details get typed into the practice management software, then again into the reminder tool, then again into the payment or financing portal. The imaging software keeps its own patient list. The phone system has never heard of any of them, so when the patient calls, the screen shows a number and nothing else.

The cost is not just the retyping. Data entered three times drifts three ways. The reminder tool confirms an appointment the schedule already moved. The imaging archive holds scans the chart does not point to. Month-end reporting turns into an argument between systems about which number is right, and staff stop trusting all of them.

Most of this is not a buying problem. The systems in a typical office can share data. They were simply never connected, because the PMS vendor sells software, the phone company sells phones, and nobody's job includes the space between them. That space is this service.

Signs Your Systems Are Not Talking

The same patient details typed into two or three systems by hand

Images and CBCT scans that do not show up where the chart expects them
A ringing phone that shows a number, not the patient who is calling

A new AI tool that can answer questions but cannot book or update anything
What We Connect

The Systems We Integrate

Integration projects start from the software already in your office. These are the connections dental practices ask us to build, repair, and maintain.

Practice Management Software

Dentrix, Open Dental, Eaglesoft, and CareStack are the systems of record in most offices. We connect the rest of your stack to the PMS, in the direction each workflow needs, so the schedule and the patient record stay the source of truth instead of one copy among several.

Imaging and CBCT

Imaging platforms hold the pictures; the PMS holds the chart. We set up and repair the bridges between them so images land on the right patient, in the right chart, without staff exporting and importing files by hand or keeping a second patient list in the imaging software.

Phones and Screen-Pop

When a patient calls, the front desk should see who it is before anyone says hello. We connect your VoIP phone system to the PMS so the caller's record opens with the call, and call notes attach to the chart instead of living on a sticky note.

Patient Engagement and Reminders

A reminder tool is only as accurate as the schedule data it reads. We connect engagement platforms to your PMS so confirmations, recalls, and two-way texts reflect the real schedule, including the same-day changes that manual exports always miss.

Scheduling, Payments, and Reviews

Online booking that writes into the real schedule instead of a side calendar. Payment and financing tools that post to the ledger instead of a spreadsheet. Review requests that go to actual patients of record. We wire each of these into the PMS so the office stops reconciling them by hand.

Reporting and Cross-Office Data

Groups running more than one office, or more than one PMS, need numbers that roll up. We build scheduled syncs and combined reporting so leadership sees production, collections, and schedule data across locations without someone exporting from each system every Friday.

Every connection above runs across your office network. If the network is slow or segmented wrong, integrations fail in ways that look like software bugs, which is why our dental network infrastructure work designs for imaging and sync traffic from the start.

Our Approach

Connected the Way the Vendors Allow

Every pair of systems has a right way to connect, and the right way is the one the vendors support. We start with official routes: documented vendor APIs, including the Open Dental API, and the developer and partner programs the PMS vendors run. Where a platform supports HL7 messaging, we use it. These routes keep working after software updates, which is the whole point.

Plenty of dental software has no public API at all. Then the honest options are scheduled file syncs, built-in bridge modules, or middleware that sits between the two systems and translates. Where a vendor's program permits it, that includes writeback: putting data into the PMS, not just reading it out. When a vendor does not permit something, we tell you before the project starts, not after.

What we will not do is build on undocumented database tricks that break the next time a vendor ships an update, or that put your vendor support agreement at risk. A slightly narrower integration that survives updates beats a clever one that breaks in the middle of a Monday.

Our Order of Preference

1 Documented vendor APIs
2 Official developer and partner programs
3 Built-in bridges and HL7 feeds
4 Scheduled file or data syncs
5 Middleware bridging, including PMS writeback where the vendor allows it
AI Integration

Adding AI Tools to a Stack That Already Works

AI front-desk assistants, AI phone answering, and AI insurance verification tools are arriving in dental offices fast. The tool itself is only half the project. The other half is integration: whether the tool can actually book into your schedule, read the right data, and hand work back to your team when it reaches something it cannot handle.

FlossByte evaluates and integrates AI tools into the stack your office already runs. We are not an AI software vendor, and we do not sell our own AI product. We connect the tools you choose, configure the data flows, and support the whole setup afterwards, so one call covers the AI tool, the phone system, and the PMS it talks to. Three questions decide whether an AI rollout succeeds, and we work through all three before anything is signed.

Does It Write Back, or Only Read?

A tool that can look up an appointment but cannot book one mostly moves work back to your front desk. We check what the tool can actually write, bookings, schedule changes, verification results, and where the hard limits sit, then design the workflow around the real answer instead of the sales answer.

Sync That Runs Both Ways

One-way sync goes stale. If the schedule changes in the PMS and the AI tool never hears about it, the tool books over real appointments. We configure and test two-way sync so both sides stay current, and we document what should happen when the two systems disagree.

Coverage, Privacy, and Downtime

Any tool touching patient data should come from a vendor that signs a BAA, and that is the only kind we integrate. We also plan the unglamorous part: what happens when the AI service is down. The answer should be that calls and bookings fall back to your team cleanly, not that the front desk goes dark.

6

Documentation and Handover

You receive documentation of what connects to what, which accounts and keys are involved, and how the data flows. If FlossByte manages your IT, we keep monitoring the integrations as part of that service.
