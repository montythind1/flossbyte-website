## LinkedIn Company Page Post Draft

HIPAA compliance in a dental practice should not live only in a policy binder.

It should be visible in the way the practice actually runs:

- Every user has their own account
- Former staff access is removed quickly
- Workstations lock automatically
- Devices and backups are encrypted
- Remote access is controlled and documented
- Audit logs are enabled and reviewed
- Vendors with system access are identified
- Backups are tested before there is an emergency

For many dental offices, the problem is not intent. It is that the IT environment grew over time: a Dentrix or Eaglesoft server, imaging software, shared folders, remote support tools, cloud email, phones, scanners, laptops, and backup systems all layered together without a clean compliance review.

That is where done-for-you HIPAA compliance setup becomes valuable.
