The Problem

Why Dental Offices Need Specialized Networks

Most dental offices are running on networks designed for general small businesses — consumer-grade routers, unmanaged switches, and single-SSID Wi-Fi that was never engineered for the demands of modern dental technology. That worked when practices only needed email and scheduling. It does not work when every operatory is capturing digital radiographs and streaming high-resolution CBCT volumes to a central PACS server.

A single panoramic X-ray image can exceed 20 MB. A full-mouth CBCT scan regularly surpasses 100 MB. Multiply that across six or eight operatories running simultaneously during peak patient hours, add the Dentrix or Eaglesoft database transactions happening at every workstation, and layer on VoIP phone traffic and cloud-based insurance verification — a consumer network simply cannot keep up. The result is slow image loading, frozen practice management screens, dropped VoIP calls, and frustrated staff who waste valuable chair time waiting on technology.

Beyond performance, HIPAA demands network segmentation. Guest Wi-Fi cannot share the same broadcast domain as your practice management server. Imaging devices containing ePHI must be isolated from general internet traffic. Without proper VLAN architecture, a single compromised device — a patient's phone, a vendor laptop, or an unpatched workstation — can provide lateral access to your entire clinical environment. FlossByte builds networks that solve both problems: enterprise performance and regulatory compliance, purpose-built for dental.

We typically create separate VLANs for your Dentrix or Eaglesoft server and workstations, imaging devices and PACS, VoIP phones, staff internet access, and guest Wi-Fi. This isolation means a compromised guest device cannot reach your practice management database or patient imaging data. HIPAA auditors specifically look for network segmentation as evidence of proper technical safeguards — and without it, you are at significantly higher risk during a breach investigation.
