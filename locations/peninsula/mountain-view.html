Mountain View Practices Run on Two Kinds of IT

Mountain View packs a lot into a few square miles. Downtown runs along Castro Street, where practices have served the same families for decades. El Camino Real carries the medical and dental corridor past El Camino Hospital. On the north side sit the Google campus around Charleston Road and Amphitheatre Parkway and neighborhoods like Shoreline West and Monta Loma, with Cuesta Park and Old Mountain View in between. Dental offices here draw from all of it, plus patients from Los Altos, Sunnyvale, and Palo Alto who cross city lines without thinking about it.

The practice mix splits two ways. Long-established offices often run Dentrix or Eaglesoft on a local server that predates their CBCT unit, tucked into whatever closet the suite had available. Newer offices lean cloud-first: browser-based practice management, imaging that never touches a local database. Both models work. Both fail the same way when the network underneath is an afterthought, with slow image loads, dropped connections at the front desk, and backups nobody has tested.

FlossByte is based in San Jose, roughly 20 minutes south on US-101, and that distance shapes how we work. On-site visits are planned runs, not scrambles: equipment staged and configured before the trip, server swaps and migrations cut over after the last patient, imaging tested before the doors open the next morning, and the first morning after a change covered on-site or remotely. Offices running an on-prem server get the Sophos standard; practices that have moved their practice management to the cloud get UniFi. Day to day, support is remote, dental-specific, and handled by people who know your software.
