The Monday Morning IT Check for Dental Front Desks: 10 Minutes Before the First Patient

Most Monday morning failures are small, and visible the moment someone checks. This is the ten-minute routine that catches them before the first patient arrives.

Monday, 7:45 a.m. The first patient arrives at 8:00. The front desk logs in and the schedule will not load. The phones show no dial tone. The printer meant to print the day sheet blinks an error nobody saw Friday.

Most Monday failures are small and visible the moment someone checks. Ten minutes before the doors open catches almost all of them in time to fix them.

1. Start with the server room, not the front desk

Walk to the server first. Check three things: the server is on with steady lights, the network switch shows lights on connected ports, and the modem or router is online.

If the server is off, beeping, or showing a red or amber warning light, stop. Do not restart it and hope. A server that shut down over the weekend did so for a reason, and a blind reboot can turn a recoverable problem into a corrupted database. Call IT and describe exactly what you see.

If the internet is down, check that the modem and router have power and that no cable came loose during weekend cleaning.

2. Open the schedule on every front desk workstation

Log into each front desk computer and open the practice management system. Dentrix, Open Dental, or Eaglesoft, it does not matter which. What matters is that today's schedule loads completely, with appointments, providers, and operatories in place.

Confirm the overnight work ran: reminders went out and eligibility checks came back. A reminder batch that failed silently means empty chairs you only discover one no-show at a time.

If the system opens on one workstation and not another, note which one. That detail saves IT twenty minutes of guessing.

3. Pick up the phones

Lift every handset and confirm dial tone. Place one test call out, and call the office from a cell phone to confirm incoming calls ring through. Listen to weekend voicemails now, not between patients, and confirm the phones switched out of after-hours mode. A phone still playing the weekend greeting at 9 a.m. tells every caller the office is closed.

If the phones run on your internet connection, a phone problem and an internet problem are usually the same problem. Fix the internet first. We have a separate guide to dental VoIP phone systems for offices still sorting out how their phones should be set up.

4. Printers, scanners, and the card terminal

Print the day sheet. If it prints cleanly, the printer, the network path, and the workstation all work. If it does not, you found the problem before the first patient needs a form.

Test the scanner with one document and the card terminal with a small authorization. A terminal that stopped talking to the network over the weekend is a checkout line problem, and checkout lines are where Mondays go sideways.

5. Imaging in every operatory

In each operatory, open the imaging software and confirm it connects, the sensor or camera responds, and yesterday's images open. A sensor that worked Friday and fails Monday usually means a loose USB connection, a driver that updated over the weekend, or a workstation that lost its mapping to the imaging server. Quick fixes at 7:50. A backed-up schedule at 9:30 with a patient in the chair.

6. Check the weekend backup job

Thirty seconds, and most offices skip it. Did last night's backup complete? A job that failed Friday and Saturday night means the practice ran unprotected all weekend. Somebody should own the backup report every morning; a failed job gets a call, not a shrug. We wrote separately about how dental backup and recovery actually works and how to tell whether yours would restore.

  • Server on, switch lit, modem and router online
  • Schedule loads on every front desk workstation; reminders and eligibility checks ran overnight
  • Every handset has dial tone; one test call out and one call in; voicemails heard; after-hours mode off
  • Day sheet prints; scanner works; card terminal authorizes
  • Imaging connects in every operatory; sensor responds; yesterday's images open
  • Last night's backup job completed

When something fails: three rules

First, write down the exact error message and which computer showed it. A photo of the screen works. "It is not working" costs IT a round trip of questions.

Second, do not reboot the server. Restarting a workstation is fine. Restarting the server without knowing why it is down is how a bad Monday becomes a lost week.

Third, call IT before the first patient arrives, not after the third one complains. Keep a printed day sheet as a fallback. If the schedule will not load, a paper list keeps the front desk moving while the problem gets fixed.

Make it a habit, not a hero effort

Print this check as a one-page sheet and tape it to the front desk. Give it one owner each week and rotate. The owner initials it and notes anything odd, even if it fixed itself. A log of small oddities is how patterns show up: the workstation that drops off the network every Monday, the printer that needs a restart twice a week. Those are the early warnings. Ignored, they become the emergency call.

Ten minutes on Monday morning buys a quiet day. Skip it, and the office pays for the same ten minutes later, with interest, in front of patients.
