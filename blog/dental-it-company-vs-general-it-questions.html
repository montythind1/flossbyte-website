Dental IT Company vs General IT: 7 Questions to Ask Before You Sign

The wrong IT provider learns your dental software on your dime. These seven questions separate a true dental IT company from a generalist, before you're locked into a contract.

Most dental practices don't choose the wrong IT company because they didn't do research. They choose wrong because they asked the wrong questions, or asked the right provider the questions that matter for a law firm instead of a dental office.

A general IT company can be excellent at supporting standard business technology. But dental practices run on specialized software, imaging hardware, and HIPAA-regulated workflows that punish guesswork. If you're evaluating providers now, see our broader comparison of dental IT support vs general IT support: then bring these seven questions to every sales call.

📌 How to use this list: Ask every candidate the same seven questions, in the same order. Write down the answers. The providers worth hiring answer quickly and specifically; the ones to avoid hedge, generalize, or promise to "look into it."

1. Do you specialize in dental practices, or are we one of many industries you serve?

Why it matters: An IT company that serves restaurants, real estate offices, and dental practices splits its expertise across industries with nothing in common. Dental IT is a specialty: practice management databases, imaging bridges, sensor drivers, and HIPAA workflows behave nothing like a restaurant POS system.

✅Good answer: "Dental is all we do" or "dental is our primary vertical", with specific examples of dental environments they manage today.
🚩Red flag: "We support all kinds of small businesses" with no dental-specific processes, or dentistry mentioned only as a bullet point on a long client list.

2. Which practice management and imaging systems do you support?

Why it matters: When Dentrix throws a database error at 8am or a Dexis sensor stops communicating with the operatory workstation, you need someone who has fixed that exact problem before, not someone opening a support article for the first time. Name your systems (Dentrix, Eaglesoft, Open Dental, Dexis, Apteryx, Carestream) and listen for fluency, not familiarity.

✅Good answer: Immediate, specific answers about your software, versions they support, common issues they resolve, how they coordinate with the software vendor when it's a vendor-side bug.
🚩Red flag: "We can learn any software" or vague assurances without naming a single dental application. You're about to pay for their education.

3. Will you sign a Business Associate Agreement (BAA) before touching our systems?

Why it matters: Under HIPAA, any vendor with access to protected health information, including your IT company remoting into your server, is a Business Associate, and you're required to have a signed BAA with them. This isn't optional paperwork; it's federal law.

✅Good answer: "Yes. We sign a BAA with every dental client before any work begins." They should have one ready, not draft one after you ask.
🚩Red flag: "What's a BAA?" or "we can look into that." If they don't know what a Business Associate Agreement is, they don't understand HIPAA, full stop.

4. How do you handle HIPAA compliance, is it built in, or billed as an extra?

Why it matters: HIPAA compliance for a dental office isn't a one-time checklist. It's encryption, access controls, audit logging, workstation auto-logoff, risk assessments, and documentation, maintained continuously. Some providers treat compliance as an upsell; dental-specialized providers build it into the service.

✅Good answer: Specific safeguards named without prompting, encryption at rest and in transit, unique user IDs, audit logs, periodic risk assessments, included in the standard plan.
🚩Red flag: HIPAA mentioned only as "we take security seriously," or compliance offered as a separate paid add-on to a basic support plan.

5. What's your guaranteed response time when our systems go down during patient hours?

Why it matters: A down server, a dead front-desk workstation, or a failed internet connection during patient hours costs a dental practice real production, often thousands per hour in rescheduled appointments. "We'll get to it today" is not a response plan. You need a contractual SLA with teeth.

✅Good answer: A written SLA with specific response times for critical issues during business hours, and an explanation of how emergencies are triaged ahead of routine tickets.
🚩Red flag: No SLA, or dental emergencies queued like any other small-business ticket. Ask what happens at 7:50am before your first patient, the answer tells you everything.

See what dental downtime actually costs to calibrate how much response time is worth.

6. How are our backups handled, and when did you last test a full restore?

Why it matters: Almost every IT company will say "yes, we back up your data." Far fewer test whether those backups actually restore, including the practice management database and imaging archives, not just files. An untested backup is a hope, not a plan.

✅Good answer: They describe what's backed up (PMS database, imaging, shared files), where copies live (on-site + off-site), retention periods, and a regular restore-testing schedule, with documentation you can see.
🚩Red flag: "Your data is backed up to the cloud" with no detail on what's included, no restore tests, or no answer when you ask when the last test ran.

Our dental data backup and recovery guide details what a complete backup strategy covers.

7. Is your pricing flat-rate or hourly, and what exactly is included?

Why it matters: Hourly break-fix billing means every support call starts a meter, including the hours a generalist spends learning your dental software. Flat-rate managed IT gives you predictable monthly costs and removes the hesitation to call when something seems off. Either way, get the inclusions in writing: remote support, on-site visits, after-hours coverage, projects, and compliance work.

✅Good answer: A clear flat monthly rate with a written list of what's included and what counts as a separate project. No surprise invoices.
🚩Red flag: Vague hourly rates with no estimate ranges, or a "flat rate" with so many exclusions that most real work bills extra.

FAQ: Choosing a Dental IT Company

What should a dental practice ask an IT company before signing?

Ask about dental specialization, which practice management and imaging systems they support, whether they will sign a BAA, how HIPAA compliance is handled, guaranteed response times during patient hours, backup testing procedures, and whether pricing is flat-rate or hourly.

Should a dental office use a dental IT company or a general IT company?

A dental IT company understands Dentrix, Eaglesoft, Open Dental, imaging systems, HIPAA safeguards, and the cost of dental downtime. A general IT company can handle basic computers and networks but often learns dental software during the ticket, which slows resolution.

Will an IT company sign a BAA with our dental practice?

Any IT vendor with access to your systems and patient data must sign a Business Associate Agreement under HIPAA. A dental-specialized IT company will have a BAA ready before work begins; hesitation here is a red flag.

Is flat-rate or hourly IT pricing better for dental practices?

Flat-rate managed IT gives dental practices predictable monthly costs and removes the hesitation to call for support. Hourly break-fix billing can penalize you for the provider's learning curve on dental software.

Conclusion

The difference between a dental IT company and a general IT company isn't marketing. It's what happens at 8am when your schedule is full and something breaks. Ask these seven questions, write down the answers, and hire the provider whose answers are specific, immediate, and dental through and through.
